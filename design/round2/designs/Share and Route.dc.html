7a The avatar on the card

The card is drawn on a canvas at 1080 × 1350, and the avatar is painted into it pixel by pixel at a whole-number scale, so it stays crisp and never taints the canvas. In Route it has a fixed spot beside the stats, on the same baseline. On your own photo every element can be added, removed and dragged: the title, time, stats, date, route and avatar. Each one snaps to the margins, the centre and a third of the way down. Pinch any element to resize it. The avatar resizes in whole-pixel steps so it stays crisp. In the design preview, scrolling over the card does the same. Text on a photo uses the Halo theme so it holds on any image. The wordmark stays fixed in the bottom corner. Both layouts take an optional title of up to 24 characters. Both layouts come in feed 4:5 (1080 × 1350) and story 9:16 (1080 × 1920). Time out is a stat like the others, the same size and with its own label. The route layout draws the night on the real map, with the route and stops over Esri’s dark tiles and the text on black below. Save for video exports every element as its own see-through PNG, either at full frame so the layers stack in place, or cropped to each element.

7b Walking the route

At map scale the full avatar would be a smudge, so it has a 12 × 16 sprite drawn from the same colour slots. It keeps the hair colour, the top colour and the eyes, and hoods keep their colour. The eyes sit one pixel towards the direction of travel, so mirroring the sprite shows which way it is walking. It replaces the white position dot and sits on the same soft halo, so it still reads as you. On the night’s history the slider moves it along the route. It walks a four-frame cycle while you drag, faces the way it is going, and stands still when you let go. Stops are pink ticks on the track, and the stop name shows when the sprite reaches one.
