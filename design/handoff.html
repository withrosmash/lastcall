Last Call

A solo night-out tracker. Start a session, log what you drink, keep water honest, watch the route draw itself across the map, then turn the whole thing into a card worth posting. Runs natively on Android so tracking survives a locked phone in a pocket. No accounts, no server, nothing leaves the device.

01 — Foundation

Color

Committed dark. The ground is true black so the screen disappears in a dark room and OLED pixels stay off. Mint is the only affirmative action color and appears exactly once per screen. Pink belongs to drinks and nothing else. Amber is reserved for hydration falling behind — it must never be decorative, or the one moment it matters stops reading as a warning.

Rules that are easy to break by accident

  • One mint element per screen. If a screen needs two primary actions, one of them isn't primary.
  • Pink is a data color, not an accent. It marks drink counts. It never becomes a button fill or a border for decoration.
  • Amber only appears when hydration is actually behind. No amber in a resting state.
  • Text on mint is #04342C, never black. Pure black on mint reads harsh and fails the tone of the rest of the palette.
  • Never a pure grey for body copy. #6B6B6B on #000 is the floor at 14px and above; below that, step up to #8A8A8A.

02 — Foundation

Type and space

The system sans, set tight and heavy. Condensed webfonts were considered and rejected: the app has to boot offline from a service worker, and a font that fails to load silently would wreck every layout. Character comes from weight, negative tracking on numerals, and wide letter-spacing on small caps labels instead.

Safe areas. Every screen adds env(safe-area-inset-bottom) to its bottom padding and env(safe-area-inset-top) to the top. Android gesture-nav bars sit right where the primary action wants to be, and this is the single most common way a layout that looks right in a browser breaks on a real handset.

04 — Flows

Thirteen screens

Shown at production fidelity with real copy and real numbers. Screens 01 through 12 are the app; screen 13 is Android-specific and exists because background location is the one permission the OS makes users grant in a separate trip to system settings.

05 — Output

The share card

The artefact that leaves the app, so it carries the identity on its own with no chrome around it. Drawn with the Canvas 2D API rather than screenshotting the DOM — compositing a live map would pull in cross-origin tiles, taint the canvas, and make export throw. Two modes, three layouts.

07 — Handoff

What to change, and where it lands

The build reads every colour, size and radius from custom properties in a single stylesheet. Changing the palette or the type scale is a handful of lines, not a refactor. These are the things most worth pushing on.

  • The wordmark. Set as plain type here as a placeholder. It's the one element on every share card, so it's the highest-leverage thing to draw properly.
  • Mint versus pink weighting. Currently mint carries all action and pink carries only drink counts. Worth testing whether pink should own more, given drinks are the thing people actually post about.
  • The preset card. It'll be the most-seen surface by a wide margin. Layout, crop and hierarchy are all open.
  • Timer treatment. A system sans at 44/700 is functional but it's the hero of screen 02 and could carry far more character.
  • Empty and first-run states. Specified in copy but not yet designed.
  • The icon. Needs 192, 512, 512-maskable and an adaptive Android foreground layer. Nothing exists yet.

Constraints worth knowing before redesigning anything.

Dark ground is load-bearing, not stylistic — the app is used in dark rooms and runs GPS for six hours, so a light theme costs real battery on OLED. Type is the system sans on purpose: the app boots offline from a service worker, and a webfont that fails to load silently would break every layout. Android requires a permanent foreground-service notification while tracking, and it cannot be hidden or restyled. Every tap target stays at 44px or above, because this is operated by people who have been drinking.
